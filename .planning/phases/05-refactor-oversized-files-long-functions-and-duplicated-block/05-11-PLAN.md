`src/services/napplet-shell/*`, and divide the resource service internally so its oversized
function is genuinely resolved.

behind. This is the bulk of that work — roughly 780 lines of module-level functions that already
sit at clean seams, so most of it is relocation rather than redesign.

Output: five service modules and a provider reduced to its component and modals.

## Artifacts this phase produces

New modules under `src/services/napplet-shell/`:

- **`intent-service.ts`** — the intent payload coercion, the installed-handler lookup, the
  candidate, availability and failure builders, the preference matcher, and the intent service
  factory.
- **`common-actions.ts`** — the first-value helper, the identity profile and follows readers, the
  public-key and event-id normalisers, the profile pointer builder, the common publish, the common
  profile and follows readers, the follow change, the reaction, and the report draft builder and
  report action.
- **`upload-service.ts`** — the blob-to-file helper and the upload service factory.
- **`resource-service.ts`** — the base64 encoder, the request identifier reader, the error
  responder, the request key builder, the content-length reader, the bounded-concurrency mapper,
  the three resource limit constants, and the resource service factory **divided into its internal
  parts** (see Task 2).
- **`adapter.ts`** — the disabled-domain list, the signer helper and the adapter factory, acting as
  the composition root.

Whether the signer helper lives in the adapter or in its own module is the executor's choice per
D-08's discretion clause; the requirement is a single owner.

  <action>
Move the resource region into `resource-service.ts`, taking the three limit constants and all six
helpers with it. The base64 encoder sits eight lines above the factory's line range but is called
only from inside it.

Then divide the factory. This is the part that needs care: the factory is 162 lines against a
plain-function budget of 80, so relocating it unchanged would carry the finding into the new file
rather than clearing it. D-12 records that this finding is resolved by the split, which means the
split has to actually reduce it — moving it is necessary but not sufficient.

Divide it along its own internal seams: the permission check that decides whether a request is
allowed, the single-resource fetch, and the incoming-message handler that parses a request and
dispatches the bounded-concurrency fetch. Each becomes its own function in this module, with the
factory reduced to wiring them together over the shared options. Verify by rescan that every
resulting function is under budget.

Move the permission check as an atomic unit. It allows a request when the napplet holds the
approved capability or when the requested origin is one of the configured upload origins. Do not
restructure that boolean, reorder its operands, or "simplify" it while relocating — it is the gate
deciding whether a sandboxed app may pull bytes from an arbitrary origin, and it is the single
highest-value line in this plan to get wrong.

Read the window identity registry through the accessors plan 05-10 created. Do not import a raw
map and do not add a new one.

Preserve the three resource limits and the bounded concurrency exactly. They cap the response size,
the number of URLs per request and the number of simultaneous fetches; all three are denial-of-service
guards, and a relocation that dropped or widened one would not be visible in a type check.

Land the verbatim relocation and the internal division as separate commits per D-16 — the move
first, then the division — so a reviewer can confirm the move changed nothing before reading the
division on its merits.
